and up to a five-figure licence, and their form accepts **"I can introduce the owner"**.
Our cards are out of their domain; a firm that owns engineering records is not.

Each of these is a *conversation*, not a submission: we cannot submit anyone's data without
their written consent, and their group policies may forbid it.

## The better move (3 hours, no code)

Add **15 targets** to the research queue in the sectors the form actually wants — solar and
energy EPCs, water utilities, plastics/packaging and food manufacturers, automotive
workshops with fleet contracts, cold-room installers. The test is one question, asked on a
first call:

> *"Do you keep records of equipment failing — commissioning sheets, warranty claims,
> service reports, calibration logs, or the control logic you install?"*

A yes is a candidate. A no ends it in thirty seconds, which is why this is cheap.

## The offer, when someone says yes

1. **They own it, we broker it.** Their name stays on the licence; we introduce and take a
   share. Their data never passes through our systems — no DPA exposure for Alphadome.
2. **Sample first.** They redact and send ~1% to Hashlist themselves; the fee covers the
   sample, so nobody works for free.
3. **NDA before anything moves.** Hashlist's own rule bars employer and NDA material, so a
   firm that *owns* the records is the only clean counterparty.
4. **Nothing is promised until a written offer exists.** No "up to $100,000" in an email to
   a Kenyan SME — the ceiling is a marketing number, the sample fee is the real one.

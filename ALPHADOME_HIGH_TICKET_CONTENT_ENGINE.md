# Alphadome High-Ticket Content Engine

**Purpose:** Turn real product work into qualified deployment and licensing conversations. Treat YouTube and short-form channels as lead generation; advertising revenue is secondary.

## Commercial Focus

**Primary buyer:** International AI/automation agencies that want to resell or deploy proven, multi-tenant systems.

**Secondary buyer:** International SMEs and operators buying a done-for-you deployment. US, UK, UAE, and European buyers are priority markets; local clients remain valuable for paid pilots and credible case studies.

**Positioning:** Deployable AI systems for revenue, operations, and scale. Lead with the business workflow and measurable result. Use WhatsApp-first operations, M-Pesa, and other African-market workflows as distinctive proof of adaptability, not as a limit on the addressable market.

**Flagship proof build:** Alpha 3.0's WhatsApp sales and checkout workflow, including catalog, lead capture, payment handoff, and operational reporting. Demonstrate M-Pesa where applicable; show the payment adapter boundary for international deployments. Before filming, confirm which parts run end to end in the target environment and label demos, sandbox flows, and production workflows accurately.

**Reusable assets to inspect first:** Alpha 3.0, the multi-tenant architecture, Scan-to-Pay, WhatsApp/M-Pesa workflows, and existing system demos/documentation. Reuse only components whose ownership, status, and deployment prerequisites have been verified; do not imply every named system is a production deployment.

**Main risks:** Payment providers and requirements vary by market; customer data access and tenant isolation must be validated; licensing must define support, updates, and reseller rights; and a compelling video may attract viewers without qualified buying intent. Validate the workflow technically, use least-privilege test data, define licensing terms, and measure qualified calls and proposals rather than views alone.

## Lead Path

1. Publish a real build log, teardown, or case study for agency owners and business operators.
2. Offer the free blueprint/checklist in the description and pinned comment; collect consent-based email opt-ins.
3. Send a short sequence: the blueprint, a relevant build breakdown, evidence/limitations, then a discovery-call invitation.
4. Route high-intent prospects to a discovery call; route self-serve buyers to the template or workshop.
5. After delivery, record baseline and post-launch measures and request permission before publishing results or logos.

Keep the call to action singular per asset: download the blueprint or book a deployment call. Do not send every viewer to a generic homepage.

## Content System

### Pillars

- **Build logs:** Screen-record a system from workflow mapping through deployment, including failures, integrations, and handoff.
- **Case studies:** State the original problem, baseline, intervention, measured result, time period, and limitations.
- **AI for business across markets:** Show WhatsApp, M-Pesa, and low-friction workflows as concrete examples with globally reusable lessons.
- **Teardowns and tutorials:** Explain one operational problem and show how a workflow/template addresses it; point to the entry product or workshop.
- **Short clips:** Pull 4-5 useful moments from each long video; direct viewers to the full build or lead magnet.

### Cadence and Sponsor Readiness

- Publish 1-2 long-form videos weekly for weeks 3-12; produce 4-5 short clips per long-form video.
- Start sponsor and affiliate applications in week 4. Prioritize automation/AI infrastructure (n8n, Make, ElevenLabs, Vapi, Twilio, Supabase, hosting, vector databases), relevant fintech/payment providers, and SME software.
- Pitch a 3-5 video series tied to actual builds, not an isolated ad read. Create a one-page media kit focused on buyer roles, geography, qualified engagement, and conversions; include audience size as context, not the headline.
- Use affiliate links only with clear disclosure and only for tools used or independently evaluated. Treat early sponsor income as supplemental, not forecast revenue.

## Proof and Trust Rules

- Maintain a claim ledger for every public statement labeled “live,” “production-ready,” “client,” “result,” or “testimonial.” Record the source, environment, date checked, owner, and permission to publish.
- Distinguish production, staging, sandbox, prototype, and concept. A successful test transaction is not evidence of ongoing production use.
- A case study needs an attributable baseline and result, measurement period, method, and client approval. If these are missing, present the work as a demo/build log, not a customer outcome.
- Do not fabricate customer logos, testimonials, usage, performance metrics, or deployments. Remove unverified figures from sales copy until validated.
- Keep tokenized ownership, investment, equity, and revenue-share claims out of the primary sales path. Obtain qualified legal review before any public promotion or offer involving securities-like rights.

## Landing Page Changes

- Lead with deployable AI systems and a concrete business workflow, not tokenization or broad platform claims.
- Show “Deployment pilots from $3,000” and “Agency licensing from $10,000” only as scope-dependent starting anchors with a discovery CTA.
- Add one approved case study when evidence and permission exist; until then, label walkthroughs as demos and avoid unsupported client-result claims.
- Provide a direct lead path: free blueprint, consent-based email follow-up, and a booking page.
- Move blockchain/tokenized ownership material off the main sales path pending legal review.

## Measurement

Track weekly: qualified views from target roles, lead-magnet opt-ins, booked calls, show rate, qualified opportunities, proposals, paid pilots, deployment close rate, license conversations, revenue by offer, and case-study permissions. Use these to adjust topics and calls to action; do not optimize for views alone.

For planning only, two deployments at $5,000 each equal $10,000 in gross monthly project revenue before delivery costs, taxes, refunds, and payment fees. This is a scenario, not a forecast. Licensing and retainers should be measured separately.
